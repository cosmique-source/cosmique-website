For classical listeners

Three recordings of a symphony are three recordings.

Most music apps insist your library is a list of albums by artists. That model breaks on the first box set of Mahler symphonies. Orbit is built on the hierarchy classical actually uses: composer, work, movement, performance.

What's different

  • Composer as a first-class entity. Browse by Bach, not “J.S. Bach” vs. “Bach, Johann Sebastian” vs. “Bach, J. S.”
  • Works, not albums. The Goldberg Variations are a Work. You have nine recordings of it — each a different performance, performer, and date. Orbit knows this.
  • Movements. Beethoven 9 is a work with four movements. Not a four-track album.
  • Performers with roles. Conductor, soloist, ensemble, instrument — searchable independently.
  • Catalog-number parsing. BWV, K., Op., HWV, D., Hob. — read out of filenames and tags. Extendable for your corner of the repertoire.
