Who it's for

For vinyl collectors

The tool you'd build yourself if you had the time.

Discogs is a marketplace. Your spreadsheet is a spreadsheet. Roon doesn't know what a matrix number is.

Orbit treats vinyl as first-class data. Every pressing tracked separately — label, catalog number, matrix/runout. Every rip tagged with the cartridge that made it. Every piece of condition language you cared to write, preserved.

For classical listeners

Three recordings of a symphony are three recordings.

Most music apps insist your library is a list of albums by artists. That model breaks on the first box set of Mahler symphonies.

Orbit is built on the hierarchy classical actually uses: composer, work, movement, performance. Catalog numbers — BWV, K., Op., HWV, D., Hob. — read straight out of your tags and filenames.

For audiophiles

Every remaster, every version.

Most servers see "Dark Side of the Moon" and call it a day. Orbit sees each pressing as its own entry, with its own metadata, its own rating — and you mark your favourite with a note explaining why.

One album, every pressing

Other servers store one “Dark Side of the Moon.” Orbit stores six.

Each version carries its own format, source, sample rate, and rating. Mark the one you reach for — and record why.
